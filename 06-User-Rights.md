# 6. Individual Rights 👤

GDPR gives individuals several rights regarding their personal data.

The major rights include:

1. Right to be informed
2. Right of access
3. Right to rectification
4. Right to erasure
5. Right to restriction
6. Right to data portability
7. Right to object
8. Rights related to automated decision-making and profiling

The European Commission summarizes these rights for individuals.

---

# 1. Right to be Informed

People have the right to receive information about how their personal data is processed.

They should receive information such as:

```text
Who is processing the data?
Why is it being processed?
What categories of data are involved?
What is the legal basis?
How long will it be stored?
Who receives it?
What rights does the person have?
```

---

# Example

A website should explain something like:

```text
We collect your email address to:
- Create your account
- Send order-related messages

We retain your information according to our retention policy.
```

---

# 2. Right of Access

A person can request access to their personal data.

Think:

> **"Show me what personal data you have about me."**

---

# Example

Rahul sends MyShop a request:

> "Please provide the personal data you hold about me."

The organization must handle the request according to GDPR requirements.

---

# 3. Right to Rectification

If personal data is incorrect, the person can ask for it to be corrected.

### Example

```text
Database:

Phone:
9876543210

Actual phone:
9123456789
```

Rahul asks:

> "Please correct my phone number."

---

# 4. Right to Erasure

Also commonly called:

> **Right to be forgotten**

A person can request deletion of personal data in certain circumstances.

But this right is **not absolute**.

There are circumstances where an organization may have a legal reason to retain information.

---

# Example

User:

> "Delete my account and personal data where GDPR permits."

The organization must assess the request and applicable exceptions.

---

# 5. Right to Restriction

In certain situations, an individual can ask an organization to restrict processing.

Think:

> **"Don't use my data for now."**

This does not necessarily mean:

> "Delete it."

---

# 6. Right to Data Portability

In certain circumstances, a person can receive personal data in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format and transmit it to another controller.

Think:

> **"Give me my data in a usable format so I can move it."**

---

# 7. Right to Object

Individuals can object to certain processing.

One important example is direct marketing.

For direct marketing, the right to object is particularly strong.

---

# Example

Company:

> "Would you like promotional emails?"

User:

> "No."

The organization must respect the individual's objection to direct marketing.

---

# 8. Automated Decision-Making

GDPR provides protections around certain decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, when they produce legal or similarly significant effects.

### Example

Imagine:

```text
Application
     ↓
AI/Algorithm
     ↓
Automatically makes significant decision
     ↓
No meaningful human involvement
```

GDPR contains safeguards for certain situations like this, subject to exceptions.
