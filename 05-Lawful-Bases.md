# 5. Lawful Bases for Processing ⚖️

## What is a lawful basis?

Before processing personal data, an organization needs a valid legal basis under GDPR.

GDPR Article 6 provides **six lawful bases**:

1. Consent
2. Contract
3. Legal obligation
4. Vital interests
5. Public task
6. Legitimate interests

---

# 1. Consent

The person gives consent for a specific purpose.

### Example

```text
☐ I agree to receive promotional emails.
```

If the person voluntarily agrees, consent may provide a lawful basis, provided GDPR's consent requirements are met.

Consent should be:

```text
Freely given
Specific
Informed
Unambiguous
```

And people should generally be able to withdraw it.

---

# 2. Contract

Processing is necessary to perform a contract with the individual or take steps at their request before entering into one.

### Example

You buy a laptop.

The company needs:

```text
Name
Delivery address
Order information
```

to fulfill the purchase.

---

# 3. Legal Obligation

Processing is necessary because the organization has a legal obligation.

### Example

A company may have to retain certain records because tax or accounting law requires it.

---

# 4. Vital Interests

Processing is necessary to protect someone's vital interests in certain situations.

### Example

Imagine an emergency where information is necessary to help protect someone's life.

---

# 5. Public Task

Processing is necessary for a task carried out in the public interest or under official authority.

This is especially relevant to public authorities and organizations performing certain official functions.

---

# 6. Legitimate Interests

An organization may process personal data when it has a legitimate interest, provided the requirements are met and the person's interests or fundamental rights do not override that interest.

### Example

A company may process certain information to:

```text
Detect fraud
Prevent abuse
Protect its systems
Maintain network security
```

But the company cannot simply say:

> "It's our legitimate interest."

It needs to consider whether the processing is actually justified and balanced against individuals' rights.

---

# Important: Consent is NOT always required

A common beginner mistake is:

> "GDPR means you always need consent."

❌ Not true.

Consent is only **one** of six lawful bases.

For example:

```text
Order delivery
→ Contract may be relevant

Tax records
→ Legal obligation may be relevant

Marketing
→ Consent may be relevant in some situations
```

The appropriate legal basis depends on the specific processing activity.
